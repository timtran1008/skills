# TRAPS

**Trap → Rationale → Aftermath → Patch → Solution.**

A technique presented as a definition gets nodded at and forgotten. The same technique
presented as somebody's mistake, the reason the mistake felt right, and what it cost them, gets
remembered — because the learner has recognised themselves before the fix arrives.

**Invoke:** `/traps <technique> <audience and context>`

## When to use it

- Any skills training where each technique needs a short, memorable arc.
- Standalone as a mini-lesson, or embedded in a larger framework.
- **Two or more techniques in one session** — one loop each.
- **One technique only → don't.** TRAPS is overhead when the session's own arc already carries
  the story.

## What it needs

The technique name, and the context: audience role, work setting, and the goal they are chasing.

## The five beats

**1 — Trap.** The persona makes the common mistake, in a concrete scene. Specific and
observable, not "communicates poorly".

**2 — Rationale.** Why the mistake feels right. The logic, the time pressure, the bias, the
habit. **This beat is the one that gets skipped and the one that does the work** — a trap
nobody would defend is a trap nobody in the room made.

**3 — Aftermath.** The consequence, tied to the goal they actually care about. What gets
delayed, lost, or quietly weakened.

**4 — Patch.** The technique, as the fix. Its steps, the **smallest possible action**, and the
trigger cue that tells them when to reach for it.

**5 — Solution.** The fix applied in this case, with one concrete example. Plus the evidence
that it worked, and the **boundary** — where this technique stops being enough. A technique with
no stated boundary gets used everywhere and discredited.

Keep the **persona consistent across every loop in a session** — same name, same role, same
situation. Different traps, one person. The recurring character is what makes five techniques
feel like one lesson.

## Nesting inside a session arc

TRAPS is the micro-loop; the session framework is the macro arc. Mapped onto
[PMLT](../pmlt/SKILL.md):

| Session step | TRAPS beat | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Phục** — opening dissatisfaction | Trap + Rationale + Aftermath | Each loop surfaces a *local* gap, keeping the tension alive across a multi-technique session |
| **Mới** — the new method | Patch | The technique *is* the patch |
| **Làm** — practise | Solution | Applied in the persona's context, with evidence and boundary |
| **Theo** — commitment | *stays at session level* | TRAPS does not replace it |

- **Do not repeat a full Phục per technique.** The session-level Phục sets the umbrella problem;
  each Trap is a local variation of it, not a second opening.
- **Theo comes once, after the final Solution closes** — never after each loop.

## Slide template

```markdown
# [Session title]
[Subtitle]

---

## TRAPS for [technique]

---

### T — Trap
- [Persona] does [specific mistake] in [context]

---

### R — Rationale (why it feels right)
- [The psychological reason]
- [The system or incentive reason]
- [One line of self-talk that sounds entirely reasonable]

---

### A — Aftermath
- [The consequence, tied to the goal]

---

### P — Patch
- [Technique name]
- Smallest action: [one or two steps]
- Trigger: [when to use it]

---

### S — Solution
- [The fix applied to this case]
- Evidence: [the simple proof it worked]
- Boundary: [when this technique is not enough]
```

Repeat the block per technique, same persona throughout.
