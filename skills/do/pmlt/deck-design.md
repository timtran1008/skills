# Turning a PMLT design into slides

Read this once the session design is confirmed and the deck is next. Not before — deck format
decided early is deck format driving the content.

## Markdown source format

Write the deck as markdown, which most markdown-to-slides converters read directly:

```markdown
# Presentation title
Subtitle

---

## Section name

---

### Content slide title
- A bullet
- **Bold** for emphasis

---

> A quote or a question, for a slide that needs to land on its own

---
```

| Markdown | Slide |
|---|---|
| `# Title` + text | Title slide |
| `## Section` | Section divider |
| `### Heading` + bullets | Content slide |
| `> Quote` | Full-bleed quote slide |
| `---` | Slide break |
| `<!-- NOTES: ... -->` | Speaker notes |

**Section order for a PMLT session:** title → agenda → Phục → the metaphor → Mới → Làm →
close. Theo belongs in the close, not in a section of its own; it is a minute of the session,
not a sixth of it.

## Design rules that come from the framework

### Title
**Don't** use the framework's own name — "Difficult Conversation Skills" tells a participant
they are about to be trained. **Do** use something conversational and memorable that names the
tension.

### The Phục section
- **Don't** open on an abstract problem statement.
- **Do** open on a concrete scene they recognise in the first three seconds.
- **Do** show the full dialogue of the typical reaction, in the two speakers' voices. A summary
  of the bad conversation does not make anyone wince; the transcript does.
- **Do** build the flow: scene → typical reaction → consequence → the hook that opens the gap.

### The metaphor
At least one metaphor slide, and it earns its place by explaining **why** the new approach
works, not what to do. A full glass. A knot that tightens when pulled. That slide is what they
repeat to a colleague afterwards, which is the only distribution the session gets.

### The Mới section
Each step gets a definition **and two or three phrases they can use verbatim tomorrow**. Give
objections their own slides rather than handling them in the speaker notes — "sounds good, but
does it work here?" is the room's actual question, and answering it from a slide says you
expected it.

### The Làm section
- Two scenarios minimum, never one.
- Each: context → what the other person says → the full breakdown of the technique applied.
- A pro tip for the pushback case — *"and what if they come back at you anyway?"*
- Show what repeating a step looks like when the first attempt doesn't land. First attempts
  routinely don't, and a deck that only shows the clean case teaches people that the technique
  failed.

### The close
Small, specific commitment. A trigger reminder — *when do I use this?* Then a punchy two-liner
rather than a quotation from a famous person. Their own words in their own mouth beats anyone
else's.
